I looked into GigSmart after the obvious question came up: is it a real platform, and do you actually need the app to use it? Short answer: it's a real, legitimate company, founded in 2016 in Denver, and the answer to the app question depends on which side you're on. Workers are effectively app-only. Hirers can use a web browser.

This isn't sponsored, and I'm not affiliated with GigSmart. Links below were checked on November 24, 2026. Rates and fees change, so check the official pricing page before you commit to anything.

## What is GigSmart?

GigSmart is an on-demand staffing marketplace. It doesn't hire you itself — it connects businesses and homeowners who need short-term help with workers looking for flexible shifts. GigSmart says it has over 2 million workers and 4,000+ businesses, covering all 50 states, across 3,000+ skills: warehouse, construction, food service, retail, hospitality, delivery, cleaning, and more.

There are two separate apps, and mixing them up is the most common point of confusion:

- **Get Gigs — for workers.** Find and apply to shifts, home projects, and part/full-time jobs.
- **Get Workers / GigSmart for Business — for hirers.** Post shifts, review applicants, manage timesheets, and pay.

The same company runs both sides, but signup, login, and day-to-day use are different for each. That's the whole web-vs-app question, covered in detail below.

## How it works for workers (Get Gigs)

1. **Download the Get Gigs app** — iOS or Android — and create a free account. You can sign up with email, Google, Apple, or Facebook.
2. **Verify your mobile number** by SMS, then add your date of birth, zip code / location, a profile photo, and the positions you can work. You must be at least 14, with adult consent if you're under 18.
3. **Browse and apply.** The app shows gigs within about 75 miles. Tap Apply on a shift that fits, or submit a bid on a project.
4. **Work the shift, clock in/out in the app.** Location access lets you clock in on arrival; without it, the requester has to confirm your arrival.
5. **Approve your timesheet, then wait for the requester to approve it.** Pay lands in your in-app Worker Wallet, then you transfer it to your bank.

There are three kinds of work in Get Gigs:

- **Shift Gigs:** hourly shifts posted by businesses, ASAP or up to 30 days ahead. Your hours are tracked in the app and paid through the Worker Wallet. Most shifts GigSmart cites pay $15–$25/hr.
- **Project Gigs:** homeowner jobs — mounting a TV, repairs, yard work. You bid an hourly or flat rate, and if hired you finalise the timeline and payment directly with the requester.
- **Job Board:** part-time and full-time positions. Applying usually sends you to the hiring company's own website or email, following their process — it's not all handled inside GigSmart.

Some shifts are **Verified Gigs**, which require opting in to a background check and/or motor vehicle records check. Opting in is free, and the check runs once you've applied and been hired for one.

### Worker pay, fees, and insurance — the fine print

- **The $1/hr fee:** every Shift Gig carries a $1.00 per hour Trust & Safety fee. It funds Occupational Accident Insurance (OAI) through the On-Demand Independent Contractors Association (ODICA). OAI is for Shift Gigs only — not Project Gigs or Job Board applications. The stated cover includes up to $10,000 accidental death, up to $90,000 survivor's benefit for eligible dependents, up to $500/week temporary disability, and up to $1,000,000 in medical expenses with no deductible or copay. GigSmart is not the insurer; the policy is underwritten by Atlantic Specialty Insurance Company, and the policy terms govern.
- **Pay timing is not same-day by default.** The "get paid fast / same day" wording is about Rapid Transfer *after* approval, not about the whole process. Workers have up to 6 hours to check and approve a timesheet, employers then have up to 3 business days to approve it, and the bank transfer can take another 5 days. Rapid Transfer (3% fee, $1.00 minimum, per independent write-ups) can speed up the last step, once the money is in your wallet. Don't budget as if a shift worked today is cash today.
- **You're a 1099 independent contractor on Shift Gigs**, not an employee — so no tax withholding, and self-employment tax is on you. W-2 shifts exist on the business side (see below), but they're the hirer's choice, not yours.

## How it works for hirers (Get Workers)

1. **Create a free Get Workers account** — in a web browser or in the Get Workers app. The two look and feel the same, per GigSmart.
2. **Set up your organisation profile** so workers know who they're working for.
3. **Post a Shift Gig for free.** Pick a category and position, set the hourly rate and how many workers you need, add the address (private until workers are hired), schedule, latest arrival time, and details like attire or tools.
4. **Review applicants, or let Smart Hire do it.** Smart Hire starts matching about 72 hours before a shift (immediately for ASAP shifts), prioritising your favourited and repeat workers, then top-rated qualified workers nearby. It backfills cancellations automatically.
5. **Manage and pay in the app or web.** Track status, message workers, approve timesheets, and pay through Stripe. Your first shift gets a staffing-support review before it goes live; later shifts post automatically.

You can also post **Project Gigs** (homeowner jobs, bid-based) and advertise part/full-time roles on the **Job Board** from the same hiring account.

### Hirer pricing

GigSmart publishes its fees, which is unusual for this kind of platform:

- **1099 workers:** 35% service fee per worker, $25 minimum per shift (or $35 minimum if a background check was requested and the worker is paid under $100).
- **W-2 workers:** 45% service fee, $45 minimum per shift.
- **Background checks** are optional add-ons through Checkr; MVR and drug screening are priced on request.

Posting a shift is free — you only pay once work is completed. There are no contracts or minimums beyond the per-shift fees above. GigSmart claims a 92% fill rate for last-minute shift requests.

## Is GigSmart safe? Is it legit?

**Legit, yes. Frictionless, no.**

It's a real company, not a scam site: Scam Detector scores gigsmart.com 88.7/100 ("Authentic. Trustworthy. Secure"), with valid HTTPS and a domain dating to 2005. Payments run through Stripe, and background checks run through Checkr. The Get Gigs app is rated 4.7 on the App Store (12K ratings) and 3.7 on Google Play (about 7.3K reviews) as of November 2026 — decent on iOS, noticeably weaker on Android.

The complaints that keep showing up, especially on the BBB, are about the *experience*, not about the company existing:

- Pay delayed for days or weeks while a timesheet sits unapproved, or while a verification issue blocks the account.
- Support is email-only — reviewers repeatedly say there's no live phone line to talk to a human.
- Verification emails that never arrive, leaving applications stuck in pending.
- Geofence / clock-in glitches, and shifts lost over them.

GigSmart's own replies on the BBB confirm the structural point: timesheet approval is in the requester's control, and approval speed varies by business. Some requesters approve in minutes; others take the full 3 business days.

Practical rules, whichever side you're on:

- Never pay to join, and never pay a "fee" to a requester or worker to get a shift.
- Keep payment inside GigSmart for Shift Gigs. For Project Gigs, know that GigSmart says it's not liable for disputes around work completed, payment terms, or project timelines — you and the requester control those.
- Screenshot your clock-in, clock-out, and timesheet. If pay is late, that's your evidence.
- Check the requester/worker ratings and read the gig description fully before accepting — experience varies a lot by who you're dealing with, not just by the platform.

## Web access vs. app — the direct answer

**No, it doesn't absolutely require Android/iOS for everything. But for workers, effectively yes.**

- **Workers (Get Gigs): app-only to sign up and work shifts.** GigSmart's help centre puts it as step 1: create a free account by downloading the Get Gigs iOS or Android mobile apps. The signup guide is the same — download the app, verify your phone by SMS, complete your profile in the app. I found no worker web signup or web login. The one web exception is browsing only: the Job Board can also be viewed on the GigSmart website at [gigsmart.com/work/g-board/browse](https://gigsmart.com/work/g-board/browse), but applying to shifts, clocking in, and getting paid are app-based.
- **Hirers (Get Workers): web works fine.** Sign up via your web browser at getworkers.gigsmart.com, or download the Get Workers app. Log in from a web browser or the iOS/Android app to post, manage, and pay.
- **Homeowners posting a Project Gig** use the Get Workers side, so the same web option applies.

If you don't have a smartphone, you can browse the Job Board and you can hire — but you can't pick up Shift Gigs.

## Bottom line

GigSmart is worth trying if you want flexible, local, hourly work and you have a smartphone — or if you're a business that needs a shift covered fast and you're happy paying the 35–45% service fee on top of the worker's rate. It's not a replacement for steady employment: there's no guaranteed work, pay isn't same-day by default despite the marketing, and support is email-only when something goes wrong.

If you're a worker without a smartphone, this isn't your platform for shifts — browse the Job Board on the web instead, or look at a web-first alternative. If you're hiring, start on the web at getworkers.gigsmart.com; you don't need the app at all.
