I looked into iSecretShop the same way I looked into GigSmart: is it real, how does the money actually work, and do you need the app? Short answer: it's a real platform, founded in 2009, and it's an aggregator — a job board for mystery-shopping assignments from many different companies — not an employer and not the one paying you. And unlike GigSmart, you don't need a smartphone at all.

This isn't sponsored, and I'm not affiliated with iSecretShop or any mystery-shopping provider. Links below were checked on November 24, 2026. Pay and policies are set by the individual companies posting the work, so check the terms on each assignment before you accept it.

## The short version

- **Real platform, real job board** — running since 2009, nearly 1,000,000 registered shoppers, from the research and technology company Prism Intelligence.
- **It's an aggregator, not an employer.** iSecretShop is the software and the board; the mystery-shopping providers (MSPs) post the work, set the pay, approve it, and pay you.
- **iSecretShop takes $0.** Whatever the provider pays is what you keep, apart from any payment-processing costs.
- **You don't need a smartphone.** Web, mobile web, and app all work — the whole point of the platform is that you can do it any of those ways.
- **Pay is all over the place.** Of the 109 shops available near me on November 24, 2026, only 26 showed pay on the card — most commonly $5, up to $75 — and the rest hide it behind each provider's contract.
- **Watch the fine print per shop.** Each provider has its own payment policy and approval standard. A pay amount on one shop says nothing about the next one.

## What is iSecretShop?

iSecretShop is a mystery-shopping platform and job board. Businesses want to know what their customers actually experience, so they pay mystery-shopping providers to send in undercover evaluators. iSecretShop is where those providers list the work: one place to find assignments from hundreds of companies instead of logging into dozens of separate sites.

That distinction is the whole thing to understand. iSecretShop itself says it plainly: "iSecretShop is a software platform and a job board. We do not pay shoppers. Our clients (the ones offering the opportunities) do."

So there are three parties, not two:

- **You, the shopper** — an independent contractor who takes assignments.
- **The mystery-shopping provider (MSP)** — the company that posted the shop, wrote the instructions, approves your report, and pays you.
- **iSecretShop** — the platform holding it all together: the listings, scheduling, report forms, and notifications.

Registration is free, you must be 18 or older, and there's no application process — you register, agree to the iSecretShop Terms of Use and each provider's contract, and you can start taking assignments right away. A PayPal email address is required in your profile in order to get paid.

## How a shop works

1. **Browse Available Shops.** Each listing shows the location to evaluate, the allowed date range, and the compensation and reimbursement amount (if any). You can filter by distance — 5, 10, 25, 50, or 100 miles — or search by city, state, or zip if you're travelling.
2. **Accept the shop.** Enter the date you plan to complete it. Some shops assign automatically; others are by request, and the provider confirms you.
3. **Agree to the contracts.** The iSecretShop Terms of Use, plus that provider's own agreement for the shop. This is where the pay terms and approval standard live — read them before you go, because they're different per provider.
4. **Do the visit, undercover.** Go in as a normal customer, observe what the instructions ask for, take permitted photos, and keep the receipt.
5. **Submit the report before the deadline.** File it in the app or on the website — photos, observations, receipt, and any required proof.
6. **The provider approves it and pays you.** Payment timing is the provider's policy: some pay within days, others on a monthly schedule, usually via PayPal or direct deposit.

## Pay, fees, and the fine print

- **iSecretShop takes nothing.** It's a free aggregator: no commission, no fee to register, no fee to take a shop. Apart from any payment-processing or withdrawal costs, you keep what the provider pays.
- **The provider sets the pay.** Every shop's reward, reimbursement rules, and approval standard come from the company that posted it. There is no site-wide pay rate, and one shop's $5 says nothing about the next shop's $50.
- **Pay timing is provider-specific.** Some providers pay within days of approval, others on a monthly schedule. All payment policies are published for you to review before you agree to do the work — read the one on the shop you're taking, not the one on the last shop you did.
- **Most providers pay via PayPal**, some by direct deposit. That's why a PayPal email is required in your profile — without it, payment gets delayed.
- **Reimbursement is separate from pay.** Some shops reimburse a purchase up to a stated amount (a meal, a product) on top of the shop fee; others pay the fee only. The listing shows both when they apply.
- **Deadlines are short.** In the snapshot below, most dated shops were due within two days of the save. This is not work you sit on for a week.

## Is iSecretShop safe? Is it legit?

**Legit, yes — with the same caveat as everything in this space: the platform is legit, but each assignment is only as good as the provider behind it.**

It's a real, operating platform: running since 2009, headquartered in Kula, Hawaii, domain registered in 2010, and the app is published by Prism Intelligence LLC. Independent round-ups score it 3.7 out of 5 — strong on fees and access, weaker on getting paid — and one 2026 review calls it "medium risk" precisely because pay, approval, and timing are set by individual providers rather than the platform.

The complaints that show up are about the *assignments*, not about the company existing: pay that arrives late or on a monthly schedule the shopper didn't expect, reports rejected on a strict approval standard, and an app that one long-time user reported stopped working on their device in 2026 after eight years of use.

Mystery shopping also has more outright scams around it than most gig work, so the rules matter:

- Never pay to join, and never pay a "fee" or "training cost" to a provider to get a shop. iSecretShop registration is free.
- Never accept a shop that pays by cheque in advance and asks you to wire money on — that's the classic mystery-shopping scam, and it has nothing to do with iSecretShop.
- Keep the money inside the stated process: provider pays you via PayPal or direct deposit after approval.
- Read the provider's agreement on each shop before accepting. The pay, the deadline, and the approval standard are in there, and they're different every time.
- Screenshot your submitted report and keep the receipt. If pay is late, that's your evidence.

## Web access vs. app — the direct answer

**No smartphone needed. This is the biggest difference from GigSmart.**

- **Web works fully.** Register, browse Available Shops, accept shops, and submit reports from a computer at home. The signup page says so directly: "You do not need a smartphone to use our system."
- **Mobile web works too**, if you'd rather use a phone browser than install anything.
- **The app is optional but useful on site.** It lets you schedule from the field and complete the evaluation — photos, geo-tags, date/time stamps — as you leave the location, instead of doing data entry later. The app is free on iOS (requires iOS 15.1 or later) and Android (rated 3.5 on Google Play, 1.82K reviews).

If you don't have a smartphone, you can still do all of it — you just do the report at home afterwards instead of at the door.

## Bottom line

iSecretShop is worth trying if you want flexible, local, small-paying work and you're good at following detailed instructions exactly — or if you want the remote surveys, which are the only part of the board that doesn't require driving somewhere. It's not a replacement for steady employment: most shops pay $5–$25, the pay and timing are set by whichever provider posted the shop, and the board near me is dominated by one gas-station chain and one repeated phone survey.

The genuinely good news, and the reason it beats GigSmart on access: you can do all of it from a computer. No smartphone, no app, no problem — register on the web, browse Available Shops, and submit reports from home.
